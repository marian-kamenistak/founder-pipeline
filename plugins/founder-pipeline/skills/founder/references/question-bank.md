# Question bank — extended

The SKILL.md asks 4–6 focused questions per stage. This file holds the extended question bank when you need to go deeper than the defaults: stuck stages, ambiguous answers, or when the user explicitly wants more depth.

Pull questions as needed. Don't ask all of them — pick the 2–3 that move the stage forward.

---

## P1.S2 — Problem & opportunity (extended)

**Origin & felt sense**
- What's the story of the first time you noticed this problem in the wild?
- Who told you about this problem in the last 30 days? What did they say verbatim?
- Why are you uniquely positioned to see this problem — what experience filters it for you?

**Why-now shift**
- What three things changed in the last 18 months that make this problem solvable now?
- What macro shift do you think is half-named in your industry?
- If this problem has existed for 10 years, why did nobody solve it well?

**Substitute behaviors**
- What ugly workarounds do customers use today? (Spreadsheets, Slack threads, manual processes)
- What internal hacks have you seen built that prove pain is real?
- What complaints surface in industry forums or Slack groups?

**Disqualifiers**
- What would have to be true for this stream to NOT work?
- What's the strongest argument against this opportunity? Can you steelman the skeptic?
- Has anyone tried this before? What killed them?

## P1.S3 — ICP + territory (extended)

**Firmographic precision**
- Funding stage range — minimum / maximum.
- Headcount range for the buyer's function (e.g., 50–300 engineers, not "growing tech companies").
- Industry vertical — and what verticals you're explicitly not selling to.
- Geography — country list + tier (e.g., DE/UK/NL tier 1; PL/CZ/SK tier 2).
- Org maturity — early hyperscaler vs late traditional enterprise.

**Buyer role**
- Exact title (with variations — VP Eng, Director of Engineering, Head of Engineering).
- Reporting line — to CTO / to CEO / to COO. Different reporting = different buying behavior.
- Budget authority — what's the discretionary spend ceiling?
- Procurement path — direct decision / committee / legal review / RFP?
- Recent hires/fires in this role at companies that match? (Signal of buying readiness.)

**Segment sizing**
- TAM source (cite per: LinkedIn Sales Nav search, Crunchbase filter, public market report).
- SAM filter — what % of TAM can you actually reach with your distribution today?
- SOM target year-1 — realistic, with current resources.
- Concentration — top 20% of accounts holds what % of revenue potential?

**Exclusion**
- Adjacent segments you're walking away from — name and why.
- Geographies you're not entering — why.
- Verticals that look attractive but you're skipping — why.

## P1.S4 — Interpreting a niche verdict (extended)

- Is the Pick carried by the evidence in the ledger, or by the owner's preference wearing the data as decoration — what single number would change the Pick if it were different?
- Was any NEAR-MISS quietly promoted because the honest Pick was uncomfortable — what's the watch trigger that would legitimately revive it?
- Which Reframe finding are you tempted to reject — and is the rejection reason evidence or attachment to the original phrasing?
- Does the Pick clear the P1.S1 money floor at the CONSERVATIVE end of the range, not the midpoint?
- If the verdict says "content angle, not a stream" — what would 90 more days of data have to show to upgrade it?

## P1.S5 — Competition (extended)

**Alternative landscape**
- Direct competitors — companies/products that solve the same problem in similar ways.
- Indirect competitors — different mechanisms, same outcome.
- "Do nothing" — what's the cost of inaction for the customer?
- "Internal build" — would the company build this themselves?
- Adjacent tools — what are they already using that touches this space?

**Competitive deep-dive (per top 3)**
- One-line on their core value prop.
- Pricing model and rough price point.
- Where they win — segments they own.
- Where they fail — segments they underserve or features they lack.
- Recent moves — funding, pivots, hires.

**Your unique attributes**
- Capabilities — what can you do that nobody else can?
- IP / assets — what proprietary stuff do you have?
- Distribution — what audiences can you reach that competitors can't?
- Relationships — what trusted networks do you have?
- Domain expertise — what depth of understanding?
- Speed — what can you ship faster than incumbents?

**Enabled value**
- For each unique attribute, what does it let the customer DO that alternatives don't?
- Translate attributes into customer-facing outcomes.

**Category**
- Existing categories you could be placed in — pros/cons of each.
- New category you could create — risk: requires educating the market.
- Recommendation: which category makes you look strongest?

## P1.S6 — Offer: differentiators + solution (extended)

**Differentiators**
- If your offer disappeared tomorrow, what would your best-fit customer genuinely lose that no territory alternative replaces?
- Which differentiator would survive a competitor reading this document — and which dies the moment they see it?
- What do you know about this ICP that competitors structurally can't know (community data, session logs, local relationships)?
- Is the territory boundary real (language, regulation, trust networks, price points) or just where you happen to live?
- Rank differentiators by "price premium it justifies" — which one moves willingness-to-pay, not just preference?
- What would you have to build/collect for 12 months to make the differentiator uncopyable?

**Offer concept**
- One-sentence offer: what is it concretely?
- Format: service / product / hybrid / platform / community.
- Delivery: 1:1 / 1:few / 1:many / self-serve / community-driven.
- Duration: one-time / quarterly / annual / ongoing.

**VPC fit (per top-2 cited pain from P1.S2/intake/P1.S4)**
- Pain reliever: how does the offer reduce/eliminate pain X?
- Gain creator: how does the offer enable gain Y?
- Specific feature/component: what concretely delivers this?

**Exclusions**
- What is explicitly NOT in the offer?
- What might buyers expect that you're walking away from?
- What's the no-go that makes this NOT a one-stop-shop?

**Value prop**
- Format: "We help [ICP] do [job] by [unique mechanism], so they [enabled value]."
- Test: if a competitor swapped names, would the line still make sense? If yes, sharpen.

**Delivery capacity (this stage owns the math)**
- Hours per client per month, end-to-end (delivery + prep + admin)?
- Quality ceiling at 5 / 10 / 20 clients — what breaks first?
- Fulfillment model when it breaks — hire, productize, waitlist, raise price?

**Specificity gate**
- Does the offer reference real names, real numbers, measurable outcomes?
- If you described this offer to 3 ICPs, would they have the same mental picture?

## P1.S7 — Service name + description (extended)

**Naming brief**
- Tone — corporate / geeky / playful / aspirational?
- Length — single word / two-word / phrase?
- Pronounceability — must work in the languages of the home market and the primary export market declared at P1.S1 (e.g. English + Czech + Slovak)?
- Domain availability — .com / .io / the home market's ccTLD?
- Trademark check — any obvious conflicts?

**Candidate generation (3)**
- Candidate 1: name + rationale + what it signals + what it costs.
- Candidate 2: same.
- Candidate 3: same.

**ICP comprehension test**
- Pick one candidate and describe it to 3 ICPs. What do they assume it is?
- Does their assumption match the actual offer?

**Tagline**
- 5–7 words.
- Captures unique value (← P1.S6 value prop, compressed).
- Doesn't repeat the name.

**Descriptions**
- LinkedIn-bio length (one line, ~120 chars).
- Cold email open (one paragraph, ~50 words).
- Sales-call opener (one sentence + one follow-up).

## P2.S1 — Economics: time + money (extended)

**Ship phase (Day 1–30)**
- Minimum viable offer — what does the offer have to do at launch?
- Internal demo customer — who's the friendly first customer?
- Build owner — who's executing? Capacity needed?
- Tech debt allowed — what's good enough?

**Pilot phase (Day 31–90)**
- Pilot cohort size — 3–10 paying pilots.
- Pricing for pilot — discounted from public, or full?
- Refinement loop — what feedback channels, what cadence?
- Retention milestone — what % of pilots renew?

**Public phase (Day 91–180)**
- Marketing launch — channels, narrative, asset list.
- Pricing — public tier table live.
- Target volume — what's the month-3 acquisition goal?
- Team — who's needed by public phase?

**Scale phase (Day 181–365)**
- Channel expansion — second / third channel turned on.
- Team build — hires needed for scale.
- B2B SKU (if relevant) — when, by whom.
- Geo expansion (if relevant) — which markets next.

**Upfront costs (one-time)**
- Build (engineering, design).
- Brand (logo, photography, deck system).
- Tech (domains, tools, hosting).
- Legal (contracts, terms, IP review).
- Marketing assets (launch content).

**Run-rate (monthly)**
- People (the founder's opportunity cost, contractors, agencies).
- Tools (SaaS subscriptions).
- Marketing (ad spend, sponsorships).
- Hosting / infra.
- Misc.

**Revenue forecast (12-month)**
- Per-month projection (M1 through M12).
- Conversion assumptions — cited or flagged.
- ACV assumption — based on Standard tier with X% Plus mix.
- Churn assumption — cited from benchmarks or flagged.

**Unit economics**
- CAC — cost to acquire one customer. How calculated?
- LTV — lifetime value. Assumed lifetime and ACV.
- LTV:CAC ratio — target 3:1 minimum.
- Payback period — months to recoup CAC.
- Gross margin — % of revenue after delivery cost.

**Scenarios + the bar**
- Target (100% of forecast).
- 50% scenario — break-even shift, runway impact, profit per founder-hour vs the P1.S1 hurdle.
- 25% scenario — kill threshold or restructure?

## P2.S3 — Pricing & business model (extended)

**Value metric**
- What does the customer pay per? (Per seat, per call, per outcome, per quarter, per project.)
- Why this unit? — tie to the offer's enabled value (P1.S6).
- Scalability — does the unit scale with customer value, with your cost, neither, both?

**Tier design**
- How many tiers? (Standard 3; 2 if simple, 4 if complex.)
- Up-tier gates — what specifically unlocks moving up? (Capacity, features, support, exclusivity.)
- Anchor price (Standard) — your hypothesis.
- Discount/premium logic — what's the ladder math? (Often 0.5x / 1x / 2x for tier 1/2/3.)

**Willingness-to-pay**
- Van Westendorp run — when? With whom? (10+ prospects minimum; low-ticket volume offers only.)
- Reservation-price asks — direct ask in ICP conversations (accounts.md names).
- Anchor benchmarks — what do they pay for the closest alternative?

**Business model**
- Subscription (recurring revenue, retention focus).
- One-time (transactional, no LTV moat).
- Usage-based (aligns with value, hard to forecast).
- Hybrid (base + usage).
- Freemium (acquisition machine, conversion challenge).
- Enterprise (custom, high-touch, high ACV).
- Partner-share (% of partner's outcome).
- Pick one. Why this model for this stream?

**Assumptions table**
- For each cell of the pricing table, what's the assumption and how do you test it?
- Required column: "Key assumption → How to test."

## P2.S4 — Viability confirmation + validation gate (extended)

**Assumptions inventory**
- List every `[assumption — validation: ...]` tag from stages P1.S2–P2.S3 (incl. P1.S4).
- For each: Impact if wrong (1–5), Uncertainty (1–5).
- Plot on 2×2. Top-right quadrant (high impact + high uncertainty) = validation backlog.

**Validation experiment (per top-right assumption)**
- Assumption: [statement].
- Method: how you'll test (LOI, paid pilot, Van Westendorp, smoke landing page, sales meeting).
- Metric: what number measures success.
- Threshold: what value = green light. What value = red.
- Timeline: by when.
- Decision: kill criteria (red) vs go criteria (green).

**Pre-mortem**
- Three ways this stream DIES in year one.
- For each: probability, what to watch for, what to do if you see it.
- Uncomfortable framing required.

**Kill criteria (pre-committed)**
- Month 3 check: minimum traction required.
- Month 6 check: minimum traction required.
- Month 12 check: minimum traction required.
- What evidence shuts this down at each gate.

**Minimum evidence to execute**
- N signed LOIs at `{currency}`X / paid pilots at `{currency}`Y / pre-sales of `{currency}`Z / waitlist of K.
- Cannot proceed to P6.S3 (synthesis + execute plan) without this gate's evidence.

## P4.S1 — Keywords & search demand (extended)

- Which exact phrases does the ICP use for the problem (from P1.S4's discourse evidence and outreach replies) — and do people actually type those into Google, or only say them out loud?
- If the primary keyword's volume is near zero: is the category too new (education play, AI-search first) or does nobody have this problem? What did P1.S4's reframe data say?
- What does the SERP for each primary keyword look like today — who ranks, with what content type, and can you realistically beat it in the territory?
- Which query would a prospect type the night before they decide to buy — and does a page answer exactly that?
- Home market vs export market: in which language does the ICP actually search for this, and does the intent differ by language? (Worked example: a Czech-market ICP that researches in Czech but buys on English-language vendor pages.)
- What seasonal window (Trends) should the launch and content calendar exploit?

## P4.S2 — Content: format + copy sources (extended)

**Format derivation (from the clients motion)**
- Channel 1 → primary asset.
- Channel 2 → secondary asset.
- Channel 3 → tertiary asset.

**Asset specification (per asset)**
- If deck → which deck type from the brandkit, hero hook, section list, CTA.
- If web → page list, hero copy, CTA flow, conversion goal.
- If app → MVP feature list, first-user journey, retention hook.
- If hybrid → primary asset + supporting assets, sequencing.

**Distribution mechanic**
- How does the asset get into the ICP's hands?
- What gates access (none / email gate / referral / paid)?
- What's the follow-up sequence after consumption?

**Dependencies**
- Brand assets needed (logo, photos, CDN setup) — from your configured brand kit.
- Tech stack — the house stack declared at P1.S1, or a recorded override.
- Tracking (product-analytics events, UTM strategy).

## P6.S1 — GTM (extended)

**Channel selection (from real data)**
- What did the outreach replies (P3.S1) and launch metrics (P5.S1–P5.S2) actually show — which angle landed, which surface sent traffic?
- What 3 channels reach the ICP at highest concentration?
- Per channel: distribution mechanism, audience size, cost per impression/lead.
- Why this channel for this ICP? (Cite observed data or benchmark, or flag as assumption.)

**Named-account list**
- The accounts from `accounts.md` (P1.S3) — status after P3.S1's touches.
- Per account: buyer role + name (if known) + warm-intro path (if any).
- Disqualifiers — any that should drop off and why.

**Sales motion**
- Self-serve / sales-led / partner-led / community-led — pick one.
- Cycle length expected (days to close).
- Touchpoints expected per close.
- Who owns the conversation? (Founder-led for first 10 deals; rep-led after.)

**Narrative (Raskin)**
- Shift — one paragraph, cited shift indicator.
- Promised land — vivid present tense, one paragraph.
- Stakes — what failure looks like, one paragraph.
- Magic gifts — 3 bullets tying to unique attributes (← P1.S5/P1.S6).
- Proof — 3 bullets of evidence with names + numbers.

**First touch**
- What's the literal opening message / first asset / first ad?
- Where does it land them?
- What's the conversion ask?

## P6.S2 — KPIs (extended)

**North-star**
- One number that, if moves, signals the stream is working.
- Tied to revenue, not awareness.
- Behavioral, not stated.

**Leading indicators (3–5)**
- Predict the north-star moving in 30–60 days.
- Examples: qualified discovery calls, paid trial signups, asset downloads with email gate, pilot conversion rate.
- Each gets a "would 50% move here predict revenue?" check.

**Lagging indicators (2)**
- Revenue (MRR, ARR, or per-deal).
- Retention (renewal rate, churn).
- Optional: NPS, CAC payback, LTV.

**Vanity check (per metric)**
- "If this moves and revenue doesn't, do I care?"
- If no, drop the metric.

**Instrumentation**
- Product-analytics events — list each (wiring shipped at P4.S3; this stage owns the metric definitions). Use whichever analytics row the house stack declared at P1.S1.
- Recurring rank/AI-citation read — this stage owns it (day-0 baseline lives with P5.S1).
- Billing events — payment, refund, churn, from whichever payment processor the stack declares.
- CRM / task board — pipeline tracking.
- Automation platform — workflow hooks if relevant.

## P6.S3 — Synthesise + execute (extended)

**Lean Canvas (compressed)**
- Problem (top 3 pains).
- Customer segments + early adopters.
- Unique value prop.
- Solution (top 3 features).
- Unfair advantage.
- Channels.
- Revenue streams.
- Cost structure.
- Key metrics.

**Raskin narrative (one-pager)**
- Shift (1 paragraph).
- Promised land (1 paragraph, present tense).
- Stakes (1 paragraph).
- Magic gifts (3 bullets).
- Proof (3 bullets with names + numbers).

**Pricing card**
- Tier table.
- Value metric.
- "What's NOT included" line.

**First 90-day plan**
- Week-by-week (or sprint-by-sprint) milestones.
- Each milestone: action / owner / due date / done-when criterion.
- Maps to roadmap Ship phase + early Pilot.

**Validation milestones (carried from P2.S4)**
- Top 3 validation experiments with timeline.
- Pre-committed kill criteria.

**Final "would I bet money?" gate**
- For each section, is it specific, measurable, cited?
- Generic sections kick back to the originating stage.
